# Core Focus Areas

## Theoretical Physics

My primary academic focus is theoretical and conceptual physics. I’m especially drawn toward problems involving dynamic systems, oscillatory behavior, resonance, relativity, and large-scale structural mechanics.

A lot of my work revolves around understanding how complex physical systems behave under instability, external forcing, and coupled motion. I enjoy taking systems apart mathematically, building models from first principles, and refining them until they begin to reflect real physical behavior.

One of the things that fascinates me most is the connection between elegant mathematical structure and real-world engineering systems.

---

## Mathematical Modeling

Mathematics is at the center of nearly everything I work on.

Over time I’ve developed a strong interest in partial differential equations, dynamic systems, Green’s functions, oscillatory mechanics, and higher-level mathematical physics. I’m particularly interested in mathematical structures that describe instability, resonance, emergence, and coupled behavior.

A recurring theme throughout my projects is trying to bridge theory with physical reality — using mathematics not just as a tool for calculation, but as a way of understanding how systems evolve and interact.

I’m also interested in the relationship between geometry, complexity, and dynamics, including exploratory work involving nonlinear systems and fractal-inspired behavior.

# Research & Technical Projects

## Dynamic Bridge Oscillations

A research-focused project investigating how dynamic oscillations propagate through suspension bridge systems.

The project combines:

- Euler–Bernoulli beam theory
- Wave propagation
- Catenary modeling
- Coupled oscillatory systems
- Wind-induced vibration effects
- Boundary-condition analysis
- Resonance and instability behavior

The long-term goal is to understand how interacting oscillatory modes evolve in large-scale suspended structures and how those interactions may be modeled more accurately mathematically.

---

## Space Elevator & Rotational Tether Research

I have a strong interest in future space infrastructure systems, particularly rotating tether systems and space elevators.

Areas of focus include:

- Vibrational stability
- Resonance suppression
- Dynamic tether coupling
- Rotational mechanics
- Structural oscillation control
- Long-range propagation effects

This work is motivated by a fascination with ambitious engineering systems that require both advanced mathematics and large-scale systems thinking.

---

## Neural Interfaces & Cognitive Systems

Alongside physics and mathematics, I’ve spent time exploring neural interfaces and intelligent systems.

I’m interested in EEG and fNIRS-based interaction systems, particularly the idea of reducing friction between human intention and digital technology. A lot of this exploration began from curiosity around how thoughts, signals, and attention could eventually become more direct forms of interaction.

That interest has gradually expanded into broader work involving brain-computer interfaces, intelligent assistive systems, and experimental human-computer interaction concepts.

---

## Smart Glasses System

One of my long-term engineering projects is a wearable smart-glasses platform inspired by highly integrated fictional systems like EDITH.

The vision behind the project is to create a lightweight intelligent interface that feels natural and deeply integrated into everyday interaction — something capable of contextual awareness, AI-assisted communication, voice interaction, and real-time information display.

What interests me most about the project is not just the hardware itself, but the larger idea of creating seamless interfaces between people and intelligent systems.
